# The NEW MAP Changes EVERYTHING (Deadlock Tier List October 2026) — Vegas (20261002)

## Meta thesis
With no balance patch since mid-September, the cast is unusually flat: roughly 60% of heroes sit in one bloated A tier, and the new City Never Sleeps map is doing the work instead. The map favors tempo — comeback mechanics are nerfed so early leads stick, and fights happen in open, less choked, less rooftop-dominated terrain. That means open-space teamfighters, clustered-jungle farmers with AoE lifesteal, and high-mobility heroes (especially ones that can reach the new pickups) move up, while rooftop-reliant or choke-dependent heroes move down.

## Map claims
- **terrain**: The new map is far more open with fewer chokes and, critically, fewer roofs sitting above flyers — on the old map any M1 carry gained high ground over a flyer just by existing, which is no longer true.
- **objectives**: Three new center/safe objectives exist (described as 'three centers in the tower'), and the hero who can rotate a team onto all three smoothly (The Doorman for CC, Paige for safety) gains real value. (three objectives)
- **pickups**: There are healing bats in the sky that heal 10% of max HP; Celeste and especially Lash have the best access to them and almost nobody is using them yet. (36 bats; 10% max HP heal each)
- **neutrals**: New jungle camps are clustered, so AoE clear and lifesteal-style heals are multiplied: Venator cleaves clusters with his 2, Rem chains her heal across them effortlessly, and Mina can kite camps together and rake them for far more healing than intended. (~1,000 HP heal per camp for Mina; ~1,500 from a multi-camp rake; 'three times more than it should')
- **economy**: Comeback mechanics were nerfed, so early snowballing now has real meaning and lane-phase strength matters more — this is the single biggest systemic shift in the patch.
- **economy**: The new Sinners are a large gold swing that a hero like Rem converts efficiently, adding to the map's tempo bias.
- **economy**: Stat-based armor and magic resist scale with farm — on high-HP farmers like Mo & Krill and Venator, farming safes yields small but real free resistances. (~3% each armor additional per claim; up to ~6% spirit armor seen free; 5,000 HP late-game heroes; 12 safes farmed per game)
- **farm**: Boxes/crates are abundant, boosting box-clearing economy heroes like Calico, and heavy melee gives jungle value to heroes like Lady Geist.
- **map**: The new Chinatown Tower is a power position: Lash's stomping ground, and Drifter can teleport from its top onto surrounding camps and escape for free without descending.

## Reasoning patterns
- Reads a terrain change as a hero buff with no number change: because the new map has fewer roofs above flyers, Grey Talon is now the high ground instead of free food for M1 carries on rooftops — same build, 'gigabuffed' value.
- Treats a system change as a hero-specific buff/nerf pair: nerfed comeback mechanics make early duelists like Apollo stronger and delete Seven's lose-lane/AFK-farm win condition.
- Converts a PvE layout change into combat math: clustered jungle plus AoE lifesteal means Mina's rake and Rem's chain heal become free survivability, so a farm-route change is really a sustain change.
- Rates by opportunity cost rather than absolute power: Kelvin isn't bad, he's worse than 'a Rem or a Paige', so he falls — same logic applied to Seven, Victor and Vindicta.
- Uses data shape over headline number: Pocket's win rate rising ~45% to ~55% with 300 games means a skill-gated hero that 'would have been nerfed months ago' if everyone played him well.
- Uses the absence of a balance patch as the explanation for an inflated A tier: most of the cast is playable, so tiering here is about map fit, not power changes.
